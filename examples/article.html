The width you were promised

Eighty-four points, recovered.

The outer display is wider and shorter than any other iPhone screen, and Safari moves its controls to the side to keep the height. Pages get laid out in what is left: 382 CSS pixels of a 466 point window.

This page takes the whole width back. The hero, the figures and the pull quote run under Safari’s controls; the paragraphs are padded clear of them with px-safe-5, so nothing you read sits behind a button.

It still scrolls as a document. Safari’s toolbar shrinks as you scroll, the way it does on any page, because nothing here holds the scroll in an inner container.

“Pages built for phones fit whatever width they are handed.”

Fixed and sticky boxes are the exception. Safari clips them at the layout width, so a sticky bar here stops at the strip no matter what width it asks for. Pages that need a header across the whole window use an app shell instead, like the Inbox example.
